Who we are

Stride is a training and nutrition log with an AI coach. It is made by heeeelyeeee, an independent developer in Norway, who decides why and how the data described here is used and so is the data controller under the GDPR. Contact: ugleskippy123@gmail.com.

Stride is for adults aged 18 and over. It does not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you think someone under 18 has a Stride account, email us and we will delete it.

What stays on your phone

Everything you log in Stride is stored on your phone, in a local database. That covers chat messages and the photos, voice memos, videos and files attached to them, meals, the meals and recipes you save, workouts, sleep, heart rate and other readings, body measurements, symptoms, goals, your profile, the GPS routes of runs you record, and what Stride reads from Health Connect, Hevy and import files.

Your Stride sign-in and your Hevy key are kept in your phone's secure storage.

The coach also keeps a notebook on you, on your phone: up to 30 short lines under Goal, Food, Training and week, Health (such as injuries, conditions and what a doctor or physio said), Preferences, Life, Agreed and Open. Google's Gemini keeps it up to date from your chat messages, and it goes to Anthropic with every message you send the coach, as described below. You can read it in Settings → Preferences → Coach chat. It stays when you clear the chat. Only Clear notebook there, or Delete all my data, removes it.

Stride also keeps on your phone: the date and version of the agreement you gave on the first screen; your reports on coach replies, with the reason and any note; and a log of which of Stride's safety rules applied in the chat and when, without the message text. Stride does not send these anywhere itself. Your reports are in your backups and stay until you clear the chat. If you delete or retry a reply you reported, Stride keeps your report and the first 1500 characters of that reply on your phone, and in your backups, until you clear the chat. It keeps the newest 50 of these.

The safety log is not part of the chat, so deleting a message or clearing the chat does not remove its entries. It keeps the newest 200, and only Delete all my data clears it. It is left out of exports, Google backups and backup folder files, but it can move with a phone-to-phone transfer. A debug report you share lists the reasons you gave, when you gave them, how many safety log entries there are and when you agreed. Your notes, the replies and the log's entries go in only if you tap Include my data.

When the coach logs, fixes, merges or deletes your meals, activities, weigh-ins, sleep, stats or a day's figures such as resting heart rate, HRV or steps, Stride keeps on your phone a copy of each entry as it was before and after the change, so you or the coach can undo it. The same goes for the coach's changes to your profile (such as your name, targets, max heart rate, height or sex), to a goal's wording or whether it is done, to a stat's name, unit or target, to a Dashboard widget's time span, title or size, to what the 1 day heart rate chart shows, including a stat it starts tracking for the chart, and to races: removing one, putting a cancelled one back on, recording one you ran that was not planned or was cancelled, and correcting a finish time. Lifting workouts, logging a score such as mood or energy (fixing or deleting one is kept), adding or deleting a goal or changing its plan, adding, changing, finishing or cancelling a planned race (and a goal that finishing one completes), deleting a whole stat, and adding, removing or reordering widgets are not kept this way, nor are symptoms, planned sessions, remembered facts or calendar events. Neither are changes you make yourself in the app, with one exception: a meal you log from the chat's + menu (from a photo, a scan, Food search or My meals) is kept the same way, together with what it adds to your stats, so its line in the chat has Undo. A copy holds the entry's details, such as a meal's name, amounts and note, or an activity's title, figures and notes, or the profile details, goal, race, stat or widget settings it changed. It does not hold your chat messages or GPS routes, though a note in it can hold words you wrote in the chat. When the coach deletes a meal or merges meals, the photo of a meal it removed stays on your phone until the copy goes. It stays longer if a logged meal or another kept copy still uses it.

Stride keeps each change for up to 30 days, and for up to 7 days after it is fully undone. It keeps at most the newest 200 changes, and fewer if they take a lot of space. One change can cover many entries. Copies past these limits are removed the next time Stride starts, or the next time the coach saves a change like this. Stride does not send these copies anywhere itself. When the coach makes a change, it gets what changed, and for your logs the values before and after. It gets a short description of a change when it undoes one, and with your later messages when you undo one, as described under What leaves your phone. Clearing the chat removes all of the copies, so earlier changes can no longer be undone, and Delete all my data removes them too. Deleting a message, or deleting the entry itself in the app, does not. They are left out of exports, Google backups and backup folder files. On Android 12 and newer, a phone-to-phone transfer can move them to the new phone, together with the photos kept for them. A backup folder can also get a copy of such a photo. A debug report you share says how many there are, not what they hold.

When you or the coach correct a session from your watch or another app, or a sync matches it to an entry you logged and merges the two, the session keeps what that app recorded, and up to 5 merged entries with their figures and notes, so they can be given back. This stays with the session until the correction is undone or the session is deleted.

When the coach deletes a session Stride read from Health Connect, Stride keeps a note on your phone of that session's ID, start, type, length and distance as Health Connect recorded them, so the next sync does not bring it back. When the coach logs a night's sleep or sets a day's figure such as resting heart rate or steps, Stride keeps a note of what it set and the figure it replaced, so that figure can be put back and a later sync does not overwrite a figure the coach corrected. A figure that only filled a gap gives way to what your watch records later. These notes stay after the copies above are gone and are not removed by clearing the chat. A deleted session's note goes when the delete is undone, a figure's when it is taken back or undone, and all of them with Delete all my data. Once a delete can no longer be undone, the session stays deleted in Stride until you use Delete all my data, though Health Connect still has it. The notes are left out of exports, Google backups and backup folder files, a phone-to-phone transfer on Android 12 and newer can move them, and a debug report holds them only if you tap Include my data.

When a workout is deleted, by you, the coach, a change to a plan or a Hevy sync, Stride keeps a short note of it on your phone: its title, when it was deleted, by whom, and the workout that replaced it, if any. That way an older Open workout button in the chat can say what happened. Stride keeps these notes for up to 120 days, and at most the newest 200. Clearing the chat does not remove them. Delete all my data does. They are left out of exports, Google backups and backup folder files. A phone-to-phone transfer on Android 12 and newer can move them, and a debug report holds them only if you tap Include my data.

Workout notification. From your first ticked set until you finish the workout, for up to 6 hours, Stride shows an ongoing notification. It is on by default. It shows the next exercise, the weight and reps of the set and whether it is a warm-up (last time's numbers where you left one empty), the workout clock, and the rest countdown or the time left in a paused rest. When a rest or timer ends, a separate alert names the next exercise with its weight and reps. Stride builds both on your phone and does not send them anywhere. It marks them for this phone only, but an app you have given notification access, such as a watch app, can still read them.

During a workout, the text of both is also kept in a small store on your phone. Stride clears the notification's text when you finish or discard the workout, turn the notification off, or 6 hours after your first set, and the alert's text when the rest ends. Swiping the notification away does not clear it. If your phone restarts or Stride is force stopped during a workout, the text stays until you next open Stride. The store is left out of Google backups, backup folder files, exports and phone-to-phone transfers. The notification stays when you close Stride. Unless your lock screen hides notification content, it can show both in full, so anyone who can see your phone can read them, and anyone holding it can use the notification's buttons, such as Complete set, without unlocking it. If you hide sensitive notification content in Android's settings, the lock screen shows only "Workout in progress" or "Rest over". To turn the notification off, go to Settings → Preferences → Workouts → Workout notification and choose Off. The end-of-rest alert does not depend on it: it rings only when Workout sounds there is set to All or Timers.

What leaves your phone

Stride sends data off your phone only in the ways described here. Each goes to the service named, for the purpose given. Some of these services process data in other countries, under their own terms.

The AI coach chat (Anthropic). The coach you chat with runs on Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. When you send the coach a message, Stride sends the request to Anthropic's Claude API through Stride's server, which runs on Supabase. The server checks your account, adds Stride's API key and passes the request on. It does not store or log what it passes on. What is sent to Anthropic:

  • Your message, and the photos, PDFs and text files you attach to it. For a voice memo, Anthropic gets the words you said, written out by Google (see below), not the recording. Audio and video are never sent to Anthropic.
  • The recent messages of your chat. Earlier photos, files and audio in it are replaced by a short text note. To each coach reply that saved or changed something, Stride adds a short note listing what it saved or changed, with details like names, amounts and dates, and what has been undone since.
  • A summary of your data, rebuilt for each message: your name, age, sex and height; diet preference, targets, goals, races and plans, with how you are tracking against each active goal; recent workouts, runs and meals; your saved meals in My meals, with their foods, portions and how often you log them; and when you set up a goal or ask for a plan or your week, a longer look back: a year of races and runs of 15 km or more, 12 weeks of lifts, 16 weeks of training volume, 8 weeks of eating, weight and recovery by week, and a year of the symptoms you logged; sleep, heart rate, HRV and other recovery readings, including values read from Health Connect; weight and body composition; symptoms and stats you logged; sessions the coach planned for you; facts you told the coach; the coach's notebook on you; your dashboard layout; which services you connected; your time zone and the time.
  • If Google Calendar is connected: the titles, dates and times of your events in the next 7 days. Not their descriptions.
  • Older history, when the coach asks for it while answering: workouts, activities, meals, recovery and body readings up to a year back, and calendar events up to 60 days ahead. For an exercise it asks about or builds a workout with, it can also get your history of that exercise however long ago it goes: your best set and when you did it, your best estimated one-rep max, how many times you have done it and the last time you did it with its sets, or the same for a similar exercise you have done.
  • When the coach looks up foods while answering, such as one you named without an amount: your usual foods that fit each, from your meal log and the list of foods you used before (see Barcodes and food search): their names, brands, the amount you usually have with its calories and nutrients (or a product's calories per 100 g when no amount is known), how many times you logged them and, for the closest fit, the day you last did. Never the words you typed in Search foods.
  • When the coach logs or changes entries while answering, or checks what a change would do without making it: the entries involved, with their values before and after, and for meals the day's calorie and protein totals and number of meals. It can also get the name, day and amounts of an entry already logged that looks like the same one, or of up to 5 entries of the same stat that day. When the coach changes your profile, a goal, a race, a stat's name, unit or target, or a Dashboard widget, it gets what changed, with the old name when it renames a goal, race or stat, and the old time when it corrects a race result. When the coach undoes a change, or tries to, it gets a short description of that change and what was put back.
  • Older messages of your chat, when the coach searches them while answering: up to 8 short excerpts per search, each up to 300 characters, from messages you or the coach wrote, of any date. This includes messages from before the coach could search, and from the separate chats Stride had before 2026-10-13, which are now part of your chat. Only messages still on your phone can be found.

Anthropic handles what it receives as Stride's processor, under its Commercial Terms of Service and Data Processing Addendum. Anthropic's commercial data retention policy says it deletes the inputs and outputs of API requests from its systems within 30 days. It may keep a request that its automated safety systems flag for up to 2 years, and the safety scores for up to 7 years, and it may keep data where the law requires it.

AI features that use Google Gemini. These go to Google's Gemini API, through Stride's server in the same way:

  • A voice memo you send the coach. Google writes out what you said, and only that text goes on to Anthropic with your message.
  • A food photo or a photo of a nutrition label that you add in Stride, whether with Meal photo, while adding foods to a meal, or for a saved meal in My meals, with any note you add, and a screenshot you import with App screenshot, so Google can read the food, the label or the workout in it. A photo you attach to a chat message goes to Anthropic instead, as described above.
  • A form-check video. Google reviews it, and its feedback appears in the chat as the coach's reply.
  • A meal you log yourself, or foods you add to one, while you track a stat that food can add to, such as caffeine, water or sugar, and the foods' data has no figure for it: each new food's name, amount and figures, and the names and units of the stats you track that the data has no figure for, so Google can estimate what the meal adds to them. Stride asks once for each meal, and again for foods you add later, and marks the amounts as estimates. A stat Google says food never adds to, such as mood or steps, is noted on your phone and not asked about again unless you rename it.
  • In the background, after every 4 new messages and right before you clear the chat: the coach's notebook so far, your chat messages since its last update (only ones from the last 14 days), and a short line after each coach reply listing what the coach saved or changed, and after each meal you log from the chat's + menu, so Google can update the notebook. For a symptom, a score such as mood, a remembered fact, a goal or its plan, a race, a planned session, a target or other profile detail such as your height, sex or max heart rate, a stat's name, unit or target, a Dashboard widget, a calendar event or a lifting workout, the line has details like names, amounts, dates and titles. For a change to your meals, activities, weigh-ins, sleep, stats, a day's figures such as resting heart rate, or what the heart rate chart shows, it usually says only that a change was made. Those messages can include anything you or the coach wrote. Messages that Stride's safety rules stopped are never sent.

Google handles what it receives under its Gemini API terms. Form-check videos are uploaded to Google's file storage, which deletes them automatically after about 48 hours.

Not sent to Anthropic or Google: your email address, your password, or the GPS points of a route. What you type or attach yourself is sent as it is, and the coach can find it again later or keep it in its notebook, so leave out anything you do not want Anthropic or Google to receive. Stride asks you to agree to this before you start, with a separate tick for the use of your health data.

Location, only while you record a run. If you record a run (Training → Record a run), Stride uses your phone's location from Start to Finish. It keeps recording with the screen off or another app in front, using an Android foreground service, and shows a "Recording your run" notification while it does (on Android 13 and later, only if you allow Stride notifications). Recording stops when you pause, finish or cancel. Closing Stride does not stop it, so finish or cancel the run in the app. On a phone where this does not work, Stride records only while the record screen is open and the screen is kept on.

Stride does not ask for background location ("Allow all the time"), and does not use your location at any other time. The route is stored on your phone with the run and is included in your backups. It is not sent to the AI coach, though the run's distance, time and pace are, like any run's.

Steps, only while you record a run. If your phone has a step counter, Stride reads it while you record a run, from Start to Finish except while paused, to show your cadence (steps per minute) on the run's page. On Android 10 and later it first asks for the Physical activity permission, the first time you start recording a run. Older Android versions need no permission for it. It does not read your steps at any other time. The step counts are stored on your phone with the run and are included in your backups, exports and phone-to-phone transfers. They are deleted with the run or with Delete all my data. Only the run's average cadence goes to the AI coach, like a run's distance and pace. Stride does not write steps to Health Connect. If you do not allow it, the run is recorded without cadence. On Android 10 and later you can change it in Android Settings → Apps → Stride → Permissions → Physical activity.

Maps (Stadia Maps). The map under a route comes from Stadia Maps. Stride requests it when you open a run that has a route, whether you recorded it with Stride or it was read from Health Connect, and live on the record screen while you record.

Stadia sees which map squares are requested, when, and your IP address, so it can tell where you are or were. At the start of a recording the squares are roughly 100 to 300 metres across, depending on where you are, and they get larger as the route grows. Stadia does not receive the route itself, the run's times or anything else about you. Map images are cached on your phone.

Elevation (terrain tiles on Amazon Web Services). The elevation behind a run's charts is the height of the ground under its route, read from a public terrain model (Mapzen's terrain tiles, which in Norway carry Kartverket's). Stride requests the squares of the model that the route crosses from Amazon Web Services, which hosts them as open data, when you open a run that has a route, whether you recorded it with Stride or it was read from Health Connect. Amazon sees which squares are requested, when, and your IP address, so it can tell roughly where you ran. The squares are about 2 kilometres across in Norway. Amazon does not receive the route itself, the run's times or anything else about you. The squares are kept in Stride's cache on your phone, so a run in the same area does not ask again. Clearing Stride's cache removes them; Delete all my data does not. Without them (offline, say), the elevation comes from the GPS altitude instead.

Purchases (Google Play and RevenueCat). Stride Pro is bought with Google Play Billing, through RevenueCat. Google Play handles your payment details. Stride never sees them. RevenueCat receives your Stride account ID, as your ID in its system, and your purchase and subscription status from Google Play. It also sees technical details of the connection, such as your IP address. RevenueCat gets no health data.

RevenueCat tells Stride's server when your subscription changes, and after a purchase or restore the server asks RevenueCat whether it is active. The server keeps only whether you have Pro and when it ends.

Crash reports (Sentry). When Stride crashes or hits an unexpected error, it sends a report to Sentry, a crash-reporting service. This starts only after you agree on the first screen, and stops if you withdraw. Turn it off in Settings → About & privacy → Send crash reports. While it is on, sharing a debug report also sends Sentry a short marker message.

A report holds the error message and where in the code it happened, the app version and update, your phone model, Android version and other technical details about the phone (such as memory, storage, battery level, screen size, language and time zone), the last few app events (such as the app going to the foreground or background) plus a short label for what the app was doing when Stride caught the error itself, and random IDs for this install that are not linked to your account. Sentry also sees your IP address.

Stride does not put your logs, health or food data, chats, photos, email or name in a report. Error messages are cut to 300 characters and have keys and sign-in tokens removed, but a message can quote a value the app was working with. Crashes in Android's native layer are sent by Sentry's own code, which does not apply that cut or clean-up. Their error messages are not cut to 300 characters or cleaned of keys and sign-in tokens, and they may carry more technical detail about the phone and its recent events.

App updates (Expo). Stride gets its updates through Expo's update service. At each launch the app asks Expo whether a new version is available. The request carries the app version, the platform (Android), the update channel, the ID of the update now running, a random ID Expo makes for this install, and your IP address. It does not include your account, logs or health data.

Barcodes and food search (Open Food Facts and Google ML Kit). When you scan a barcode, Stride reads it on your phone with Google's ML Kit, using a model that ships inside the app. The camera image stays on your phone. Stride sends just the barcode number to Open Food Facts to look up the product, and Open Food Facts also sees your IP address.

Search foods looks in a list of foods that ships inside the app and, when your phone is online, also searches Open Food Facts for branded products. Once you have typed at least 3 letters and stop typing for a moment, Stride sends the words you typed to Open Food Facts' search, whether or not you pick a result, and then the barcode number of a product you pick, to look it up. Open Food Facts also sees your IP address. Offline, only the list in the app is searched.

To show the foods you use first, Stride keeps a list on your phone of the foods you logged from Search foods or a barcode scan, and the foods the coach logged for you from a look-up: each one's name, brand, calories per 100 g, how many times and when you last logged it, the amounts of up to its last 5 logs, and up to 5 of the words you typed that found it, for the newest 150 foods. When the coach looks up a food, the foods on the list that fit go to Anthropic with your usual amounts (see What leaves your phone), never the words you typed; the list is not sent anywhere else. It is included in your backups, exports and phone-to-phone transfers, and a debug report holds it only if you tap Include my data. Deleting a logged meal, or undoing one the coach logged, does not remove it from the list. Delete all my data removes it.

My meals, the meals and recipes you save to log again, are kept on your phone: each one's name, its foods with their amounts, calories and nutrients, how many portions it makes, the weight of the cooked batch if you give one, its meal type and a note if it has one, and how many times and when you last logged it. All of it except the meal type and note goes to Anthropic in the summary sent with each coach message, so the coach can log one for you (see What leaves your phone). Logging one adds a meal to your log like any other, and that meal is shared as described for meals. They are included in your backups, exports and phone-to-phone transfers, and a debug report holds them only if you tap Include my data. Changing or deleting a saved meal leaves the meals already logged from it as they are. Delete all my data removes them.

Google's ML Kit documentation says ML Kit sends Google technical diagnostics: phone model, Android and app version, an ID for this install, timings and error codes. It does not list the image or the barcode.

Hevy, Strong and Cronometer. Hevy is off until you paste your Hevy key. Stride keeps it in secure storage and uses it to read your workouts and body measurements from Hevy, read only. Hevy sees the key and your IP address. Strong and Cronometer work from files: you export a file from them and open it in Stride, which reads it on your phone and sends nothing to either service.

Google Calendar (optional). If you connect it, Google asks you to let Stride view and edit events on your calendars. Stride only uses your primary calendar. It reads event titles, descriptions, times and all-day flags from Google, and shows the titles, times and all-day flags in the Calendar tab, but not the descriptions. If you turn on the Calendar layer of the 1 day heart rate chart, it also reads that day's timed events and shows their titles and times there. Stride keeps those in memory while the app is open and does not save them. It adds, moves or deletes events only when you or the coach ask.

While Calendar is connected, event titles, dates and times for the next 7 days go to Anthropic for the AI coach chat, as described above, so the coach can plan around your week. When the coach looks further ahead, up to 60 days, those events go too. What you or the coach write about an event in the chat, including the title and start time of an event the coach adds or changes, can reach Google with your recent chat messages when it updates the coach's notebook, and so can end up in the notebook. Descriptions are not sent. Stride's server does not store calendar data. Stride does not use it for ads and does not sell it.

Stride's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

To stop, tap Disconnect in the Calendar tab. To remove Stride's permission completely, go to myaccount.google.com/permissions.

What you share yourself. In Settings → About & privacy, Share debug report makes a file with the app version, phone details, database and sync status and recent errors, and hands it to the app you pick. It holds your workouts, meals, health data, chats, the coach's notebook, report notes and the safety log entries only if you tap Include my data. Then it holds the newest 50 rows of every table in your database and your settings, which also takes in your profile (name, birth year, sex, height and weight), goals, races, symptoms, things you told the coach, planned sessions and the GPS points of your runs. It does not hold the copies Stride keeps so the coach's changes can be undone, though the chat in it shows each change the coach made, with the names, amounts, notes and dates it gave. Without it, the file still lists the reasons you gave on reports, how many safety log entries there are, how many of the coach's changes Stride keeps, a row count for each table and the app's recent log, and an error message in the log or in the sync status can quote a value the app was working with. If crash reports are on, the file also holds the random ID your crash reports carry and the ID of the marker message sent to Sentry, so whoever has the file and your crash reports can match them, and with Include my data the file ties that ID to your name.

On a coach message, Share or copy opens the app you choose, and the text ends with a line saying Stride's AI coach wrote it. The exception is the fixed reply Stride itself gives to a message about self-harm: Stride wrote that one, not the AI, so it is shared without the line. Report this reply keeps your report on your phone. If you tap Also email it to Stride, it opens an email draft with your reason, your note if you wrote one, and the start of the reply (up to 1500 characters) for you to read and send. The file, the text and the email go only where you send them.

Health Connect

With your permission, Stride reads these from Health Connect: exercise sessions and their routes (Health Connect can ask before sharing each route), distance, speed, heart rate, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, VO2 max, steps, floors climbed, elevation gained, calories burned, sleep, weight and body fat.

Stride only reads. It never writes to Health Connect. It reads when you open the app, not while the app is closed.

Stride uses this data to show your training, recovery and body trends, and to give the AI coach context when you ask it something. It is stored on your phone. It goes to other services in these ways. A summary goes to Anthropic for the AI coach chat, as described above, because you ask the coach. What you or the coach write about it in the chat can reach Google with your recent chat messages when it updates the coach's notebook, and so can end up in the notebook. And map squares for a route are requested from Stadia Maps, and terrain squares from Amazon Web Services, without the route itself. If you or the coach mention these values in the chat, an excerpt can reach Anthropic when the coach searches older messages. It is also in the backups and copies described below. It is not used for advertising and is not sold.

Stride's use and transfer of information received from Health Connect adheres to the Health Connect by Android Permissions policy, including the Limited Use requirements:

  • Data use should be limited to providing or improving the appropriate use case or features visible in the application's user interface.
  • User data may only be transferred to third parties with explicit user consent: for security purposes (for example, to investigate abuse), to comply with applicable laws or regulations, or as part of mergers/acquisitions.
  • Human access to user data is restricted unless explicit user consent is obtained, for security purposes, to comply with laws, or when aggregated for internal operations as per legal requirements.

All other transfers, uses, or sale of Health Connect data is prohibited, including:

  • Transferring or selling user data to third parties like advertising platforms, data brokers, or any information resellers.
  • Transferring, selling, or using user data for serving ads, including personalized or interest-based advertising.
  • Transferring, selling, or using user data to determine credit-worthiness or for lending purposes.
  • Transferring, selling, or using user data with any product or service that may qualify as a medical device, unless the medical device app complies with all applicable regulations, including obtaining necessary clearances or approvals from relevant regulatory bodies (for example, U.S. FDA) for its intended use of Health Connect data, and the user has provided explicit consent for such use.
  • Transferring, selling, or using user data for any purpose or in any manner involving Protected Health Information (as defined by HIPAA) unless user-initiated and in compliance with HIPAA regulations.

To stop Stride reading Health Connect, open Android Settings → Health Connect → App permissions → Stride and turn off the types you want. What Stride has already copied stays on your phone until you delete it. Settings → Backup & data → Delete all my data removes it from your phone. That does not remove anything inside Health Connect itself, or the copies described under Backups and copies.

Your Stride account

Your account is hosted by Supabase. It holds your email address; a hash of your password, not the password; the date you signed up, which sets when your free trial ends; whether you have Stride Pro and when it ends; and counts of your coach requests, of the free meal photos you take after the trial and of the goal setups the coach runs on its stronger model (with when each day's latest setup started and how many requests it made), for today and yesterday, by UTC day (older counts are deleted automatically the next time anyone uses the same feature, and goal setup counts also the next time anyone's app loads their account). Stride keeps these until you delete your account.

Each sign-in session also records your IP address and a technical client label, and the account keeps the time of your last sign-in. The session records are deleted with your account. Supabase also keeps its own technical logs, which it deletes on its own schedule.

Stride's server never stores your logs, health data, chats or photos.

Backups and copies

Your Stride data is also copied in these ways:

  • Android backup to your Google account. If Google backup is on, Android copies a compressed snapshot of your Stride data to your Google account about once a day. It holds what an export holds: chats, the coach's notebook, health and fitness data, GPS routes, meals, symptoms and your profile. It leaves out photos, voice memos, videos and your sign-ins. Stride asks Android to back up only when it can be encrypted with your screen lock. Delete all my data and uninstalling do not remove Google's copy, and a reinstall can offer to restore it. To stop it or delete it, use your phone's Google backup settings.
  • Phone-to-phone transfer. On Android 12 and newer, when you set up a new phone with Android's transfer, your Stride data, including photos, voice memos and videos, can move with it. On Android 11 and older, only the daily snapshot can, and only when it can be encrypted, so photos, voice memos and videos stay behind.
  • Backup folder (optional). If you choose a folder in Settings → Backup & data, Stride saves a backup file there each day you open it, with your photos, voice memos and videos, and keeps the last 7 backups. These files are not encrypted and contain your health data. If the folder syncs to a cloud service, that service holds them too. Stride does not delete them when you delete a message or your data. Delete them in the folder yourself.
  • Export. Settings → Backup & data → Export saves a file you keep or send where you choose. It is not encrypted and contains your health data. Stride also leaves a copy of each export in its own storage on your phone, which Delete all my data or uninstalling Stride removes. Stride does not track or delete the copies you save or send.

Each of these copies holds the coach's notebook. If you restore one onto a phone that already has a notebook, Stride keeps that phone's notebook.

Keeping and deleting data

  • Delete a message, or clear the chat (Clear chat on the Chat tab): removes that message, or every message, from your phone, together with the photos, voice memos, videos and files attached. The coach can no longer search them. A file that another message or a logged meal still shows stays until that is deleted too. Copies in backups and exports stay until you delete them, and so does anything already sent to Anthropic or Google. Clearing the chat does not delete what the coach saved or logged from it, such as facts it remembers, meals, workouts, symptoms and planned sessions. It also keeps the coach's notebook: Stride first brings the notebook up to date, which sends your recent messages to Google as described above, and asks you before clearing if it can't. Clearing the chat also removes the copies Stride keeps to undo the coach's changes, so those changes can no longer be undone. It also removes the photos of meals the coach deleted or merged away, unless a logged meal still shows them. It does not remove the notes kept on sessions the coach deleted, figures it set and deleted workouts. Deleting one message leaves the notebook and these copies as they are. To remove the notebook, use Settings → Preferences → Coach chat → Clear notebook. To remove a remembered fact or a symptom, ask the coach to forget or delete it. Meals and workouts can be deleted in the app. If the coach logged or changed a meal or session in the last 30 days, the copy Stride keeps to undo that change stays until it is removed, as described under What stays on your phone. Delete all my data removes all of it. A reply you reported is the exception when you delete or retry it: its report and the first 1500 characters of the reply stay, as described under What stays on your phone.
  • Delete all my data (Settings → Backup & data → Delete all my data): removes from your phone every log, the chat, the coach's notebook, every profile detail and setting, the list of foods you used before (from food search, barcode scans and the coach's look-ups), your saved meals, your GPS routes and the step counts of recorded runs, the photos, voice memos and videos Stride stored, the backup snapshot and export files in the app's storage, the record of your agreement, your reports on coach replies, the safety log, the copies kept to undo the coach's changes, the notes on sessions it deleted and figures it set, the notes on deleted workouts, your Hevy key and the random crash-report ID. A debug report file holds your workouts, meals, health data and chats if you tapped Include my data. To remove the cache files, clear Stride's cache in Android Settings → Apps → Stride → Storage. It does not delete your Stride account or sign you out, files in a backup folder, exports you shared, Android's Google backup, local error logs, anything already sent to Anthropic, Google or other services, or anything inside Health Connect.
  • Delete your account (Settings → Account → Delete account): removes your email, password hash, plan, usage counts and sign-in sessions from Stride's server at once. It does not touch the data on your phone and does not cancel a Google Play subscription, which you cancel in Google Play. Delete all my data also withdraws your agreement, so Stride asks you to agree again before you can reach Delete account. Without the app, use the delete account page or email us.
  • Uninstall Stride: removes its data from your phone. Android's Google backup copy stays.
  • Disconnect a service: Hevy in Settings → Integrations, Google Calendar in the Calendar tab, Health Connect in Android Settings → Health Connect → App permissions. Data already synced stays on your phone. To remove Stride's access completely, also do it in that service's own settings.
